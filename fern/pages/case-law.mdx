Case law records how California courts interpret statutes, rules, and prior decisions. Published opinions may be cited as precedent; unpublished opinions generally have limited use under the California Rules of Court.

<Note>
A case summary is not a substitute for reading the official opinion and checking whether later decisions have limited, distinguished, or overruled it.
</Note>

## Official sources

- [California Courts Opinions](https://www.courts.ca.gov/opinions.htm) — opinions from the California Supreme Court and Courts of Appeal.
- [California Case Law](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/) — a searchable secondary index for discovery.
- [California Legislative Information](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/) — the statutory text that opinions may interpret.

## Reading an opinion

1. Note the court, date, docket number, and publication status.
2. Separate the holding from background facts and dicta.
3. Follow every statute, rule, and prior case cited in the reasoning.
4. Check subsequent history before relying on the decision.

## Search tips

Use distinctive party names, a citation, or a legal phrase. Narrow by court and date when the source supports filters. Preserve the official citation and link with your research notes.

This resource provides access guidance only. It does not provide legal advice or an assessment of any specific case.
